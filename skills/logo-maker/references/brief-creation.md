# Brief evidence and concept intent

Start from the supplied project, repository and owner statements. Copy the relevant evidence into
`inputs/`; `new` supplies `brief-evidence.toml`, and `brief` names incomplete records. Keep source
excerpts exact. A fixture source establishes the fictional exercise, not a researched market fact.

Record claims with source IDs and `established`, `assumption`, `unknown` or `conflict` status.
Requirements have `owner`, `project` or `proposal` origin and `must`, `prefer`, `avoid` or `open`
priority. Preserve a confirmed owner symbol/material mandate; agent suggestions remain proposals.
Use `communication_priority` for the leading intended impression and `design_latitude` for choices
left open. Describe audience behavior as unknown when only owner intent is available.

Keep workflow instructions separate in optional `[[obligations]]` records: named `id`, `claim`,
`origin`, `priority = "must"` and source IDs. `kind = "brief"` names a `baseline` brief for
clarification review before freezing. `kind = "authoring"` requires purposeful detail in the plan
and actual images; retain `baseline_image` when the request corrects a prior artifact.
`kind = "validation"` declares `minimum_references`; distinct craft images and both orders are
checked before selection. Pin `expected_references` entries with label, input path and SHA-256 so
the intended images cannot be replaced by an unrelated pair. These supported obligations retain
their authority and deadlines.
“Compare both references” is a validation obligation; it does not prescribe another brand's style.
“More detail” requires visible development, not a universal ornament quota. Keep the prior overall
rejection bound to its exact artifact in owner-feedback history.

Keep the readback short: real activity, primary audience encounter, communication priority,
mandates, latitude and consequential unknowns. Before asking the owner, check existing evidence and
write down two plausible answers and the different design decisions they would cause. Defer a
question whose answer would not change the next action. Resolve contradictions explicitly; keep
optional design choices open. A short sufficient brief can proceed.

`logo-maker brief <slug>` reviews evidence and freezes requirements before authoring. Reopen changed
inputs with `--revision-reason`; the previous freeze remains in history. A passed brief does not
establish that an image is good.

After ideation, `logo-maker plan <slug>` creates each missing concept `plan.toml`. Fill its frozen
requirement revision, visual hypothesis, visible mechanism, requirement IDs and assumptions. Each
detail names a feature, purpose, intended scale, construction and deletion test. Proportion, edge
hierarchy, lettering and credible assembly can supply detail; texture and ornament are choices.
Primary recognition contours belong in the shared flat motif. Hero construction and finish develop
that same identity at their intended scales.

Compare actual flat directions before finish variants. A name-led or nonliteral concept is valid
where requirements permit; a mark need not explain the complete business. Review in required
contexts before revealing rationale. For unresolved construction, retain a control and predict the
visible effect of one bounded change. Inspect the result before changing the diagnosis.

Plan review records proposed intent only. Actual-image eligibility independently assesses craft
against each supplied floor, own-brief fit, intended appearance and technical acceptance. Preference
and rendering-device policy remain separate. An overall owner rejection vetoes that exact reviewed
artifact without inventing dimensional labels. Preserve rejected bytes, revisions and feedback.
