# Brief process follow-up: test a question's effect on the mark

Research date: 2026-11-10. Research and proposed manual pilot only; no implementation, artwork, production Jev question, threshold or owner-label changes. This extends [brief-process-improvements](brief-process-improvements.md), [brief-creation-pipeline](brief-creation-pipeline.md) and [brief-content-improvements](brief-content-improvements.md). Those already cover evidence, freeze, three concepts, detail plans and held-out evaluation. Their process recommendations are the baseline here, not new findings.

## Finding

The next experiment should isolate **a consequential clarification followed by a fast visual decision**, rather than add more intake records. A completed brief and several distinct alternatives can still produce an unconvincing identity. Before spending on surface detail, inspect whether any proposed mark deserves development in its declared encounter. An owner can reject all directions; a portfolio comparison must not force a winner.

The additional contribution is to separate three questions: Did the interview change a decision? Did that decision improve actual output under equal effort? Did early critique detect an unpromising direction before expensive finish work? These can have different answers. Do not use more fields, questions, detail, source links or model confidence as the outcome.

## Primary evidence: useful mechanisms with limited transfer

**Parallel creation has some outcome evidence beyond opinion.** Dow and colleagues compared parallel and serial graphic-web-ad prototyping in a between-subjects experiment with 33 participants. Both conditions made five prototypes and a final advertisement, with equal creation and critique time. Parallel work grouped prototypes before feedback; the reported final outputs performed better on click-through and expert/client assessments, and were more diverse. This is stronger evidence for a possible exploration mechanism than a studio retrospective. It does not establish that three is optimal, that logo briefs improve, or that an automated geometry pipeline behaves like the study participants. Advertisement response is also different from identity quality. [Author-hosted full study, TOCHI 2010](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2010/parallel-prototyping/ParallelPrototyping2010-final.pdf).

**Comparison can expose problems without supplying solutions.** Tohidi and colleagues studied evaluation of one interface versus three functionally equivalent, stylistically different interfaces. A design presented alone received higher ratings and less negative feedback than the same design among alternatives. Importantly, conventional usability testing did not reliably elicit constructive redesign proposals, even with alternatives. For this project, ask owners to identify reactions, misfit and preference; do not demand that they diagnose geometry or invent the replacement symbol. The evidence concerns interface testing, not logos or universal accuracy of comparison judgments. [Microsoft Research's first-party publication, CHI 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/getting-the-design-right-and-the-right-design-testing-many-is-better-than-one/).

**A freeze should preserve a decision, not forbid learning.** Design Council explicitly describes a nonlinear process: discovery can redefine the challenge, early making can contribute to discovery, and testing may return a team to the underlying problem. This supports a visible reopening route. It does not supply a numerical readiness or stopping threshold. The policy below is a proposed local operational rule, not a claim that the Double Diamond experimentally improves logo acceptance. [Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/).

**Question quality depends on the conversation, not a checklist.** GOV.UK recommends preparing research topics and follow-ups, trying the guide, asking open neutral questions, concentrating on concrete stories, clarifying what was said and following useful unexpected threads. Translate that into a brief discussion about one consequential encounter before offering visual interpretations. This is practice guidance for service-user interviews. An owner interview establishes owner intentions and reported beliefs; it does not establish audience behavior. [Using in-depth interviews](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews).

## Low-complexity extensions to try manually

### P0: Use a visual decision checkpoint before more finish

The parent task reports that a fresh Rootshare development preview resembles an orange rounded ceramic plate with three almond apertures and pale collars, with a possible generic container/cheese-grater association. It also reports a surface-frequency probe that retained geometry, lighting and samples and produced a subtle surface change without obvious identity improvement. These are parent-supplied development observations, not independently inspected pixels in this research note, owner judgments or new business facts. Rootshare remains an unsettled development case, not an approved success.

For the next review, show the existing alternatives as actual flat marks plus the exact name/lockup and a declared small-use application. Keep visual treatment comparable. First record what is seen without a rationale; then disclose intended communication and ask what belongs, what confuses, and what the owner would reject. A logo need not explain the entire service independently: assess what the mark, name and context each contribute. If a treatment is itself essential to the approved appearance, include a matched treatment preview too; a flat sheet cannot decide that dimension alone.

Finish with one explicit decision: develop a direction; make one specific diagnostic revision; reopen a requirement; or stop with no viable direction. A comparison winner still needs independent eligibility. Do not advance solely because it beats weaker alternatives. Do not make more ornament the default response to an identity problem.

**Test:** compare this checkpoint against the current review sequence with equal total authoring/render opportunities. Record whether it detects the eventual rejected direction earlier, whether it wrongly eliminates a later acceptable one, and whether saved effort yields a better final image. An early rejection by itself is not improved logo quality.

### P0: Ask one question that can change the next action

Before asking, retain two plausible answers and the different authoring decisions they imply. Ask an open question before suggesting alternatives: for example, “Describe the moment someone needs to recognize this organization; what confusion would matter there?” These are proposed interview prompts, not production Jev wording. Do not invent an encounter or confusion if the owner does not supply one. “Unknown” and “designer to explore” are valid outcomes.

Then read back the answer and the interpretation separately. If no plausible answer changes the next action, defer the question. If a requested aesthetic is a real owner mandate, keep it; challenging a brief is not permission to discard it.

A lightweight challenge can use three sentences: “The current direction assumes X. The evidence establishes Y, while Z remains uncertain. Testing A against B would tell us whether to retain X.” Owner refusal, genuine fixed constraints and limited scope remain legitimate stopping conditions. Avoid turning a suggestive designer theory into an owner fact.

**Test:** retain the before/after decision and actual images. Count questions answered from existing evidence, owner corrections, elapsed effort, and decisions changed; treat all as secondary measures. A useful conversation without better output is an informative null result.

### P1: Make stopping and reopening explicit

| Situation | Proposed manual action | Evidence retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remaining unknown cannot change the next bounded sketch | Stop intake for now; explore within the frozen latitude | Unknown and why it is nonblocking |
| Two essential requirements conflict, or an absent fact determines permissible identity | Resolve that issue before qualifying outputs | Exact conflict; owner answer or unresolved status |
| All directions fail the early encounter/meaning review | Stop finish expansion; diagnose concept versus mistaken requirement | Actual sheets and rejection; no forced best candidate |
| Prototype reveals an assumption was wrong, or owner changes a mandate | Reopen the specific requirement with the owner; preserve the prior revision | Trigger, changed requirement, affected candidates and review needed |
| Candidate merely scores poorly under the existing brief | Keep the brief; revise/reject candidate unless independent evidence justifies reopening | Failure dimension and diagnostic next step |
| Time/render allowance ends without qualification | Report no qualifying result | All failures and remaining uncertainty |

No universal number of interview rounds is supported by the retrieved sources. A budget bounds the pilot; it does not convert unfinished work into a pass. Reopening creates a new revision and reruns affected review, rather than rewriting the old brief to make an old image appear successful. This preserves traceability while allowing the nonlinear learning Design Council describes. It requires no new schema during a manual trial.

## A controlled brief-to-image pilot

**First isolate the brief intervention.** On development brands, pair the current evidence/freeze workflow (A) with the same workflow plus one consequential clarification/readback (B). Keep the same available business evidence, confirmed mandates, concept count, authoring time, treatment options, review access, render allowance and eligibility rules. Both arms receive the same concept-review sequence. Adding parallel critique only to B would confound whether the brief itself helped. Record any additional owner information acquired in B: that acquisition is part of this intervention, not proof that a longer rewritten prompt helped.

To avoid knowledge leaking from B into A, freeze A before the new answer, then use independent authoring contexts with identical tooling and no access to the other arm's outputs. Authoring order or author skill can still confound a tiny trial; counterbalance order across brands and report these limits. Exact briefs, output hashes and attempts matter. Compare all planned attempts and no-qualifier cases, not just one cherry-picked winner. If both arms fail, do not report the less-bad one as a quality gain.

**Then isolate review timing.** With the chosen brief procedure held fixed, compare early parallel visual critique against the current review order, under the same total effort. This tests the Dow/Tohidi-inspired mechanism separately. Do not change interview method, rendering quality and critique timing together and attribute the result to brief quality.

**Reserve transfer evidence.** Use Rootshare only for development. Reserve an entire untouched brand with its owner's real requirements before tuning this procedure; another Rootshare treatment is not a held-out brand. Include a different intended appearance to avoid learning a Chief-like finish preference. Do not invent the brand's facts or owner labels. One held-out brand is a smoke test of transfer, not a population estimate or a causal guarantee.

For each exact final artifact, collect fresh judgments separately:

| Dimension | Evidence/outcome to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Craftsmanship | Independent comparisons against **both Chief original and Chief reconstruction**, in both presentation orders, using required whole-image and native-detail evidence; neither reference's styling becomes the target brand's identity |
| Business/communication fit | Fit to that arm's own frozen requirements in declared applications; actual owner judgment kept separate from audience evidence |
| Intended appearance | Whether that brand's intended material/form/finish is achieved; independent of general craft and of favorite reference |
| Aesthetic preference | Blind preference with ties and neither acceptable allowed; cannot compensate for failure elsewhere |
| Overall owner decision | Explicit accept/reject/unknown on the exact submitted images; no dimensional labels inferred from overall rejection |
| Operational eligibility | Render provenance/GPU compliance, image acceptance and provider errors kept separate from visual judgments |

Hide workflow labels, rationales and model scores initially; randomize order and retain responses before revealing rationale. Preserve disagreements rather than averaging craft, fit and taste into one flattering score. Model assessments are diagnostics and cannot replace owner acceptance or certify quality by themselves. Chief owner approval does not transfer to a new image. Production GPU policy remains independent: early cheap studies do not qualify final renders; observe the repository's full GPU-render timeout rather than terminating compilation.

Predeclare a practical question: does B produce more independently qualifying, owner-accepted actual outputs within the same effort, without advancing rejected or uncertain artifacts? Report per-brand results and denominators alongside effort. Retain **no qualifier**, **missing owner response**, **review uncertainty**, **provider failure**, **GPU-policy failure** and **not attempted** as distinct outcomes. Missing data is neither rejection nor acceptance. Do not tune thresholds, relabel fixtures or grant positive owner overrides to force a result. A tiny pilot supports iteration; robust causal or statistical claims need more independently sampled brands and repeated runs than proposed here.
