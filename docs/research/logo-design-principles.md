# Logo design principles — types, negative space, scalability tests

Name-agnostic background for judging and authoring marks. Sources are free editorial pieces;
quotes are verbatim from the cited pages.

## 1. Logo types

| Type | What it is | Strength | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wordmark | Full name set in styled type | Name recall | Poor for long names |
| Lettermark / monogram | Initials or an abbreviation | Shortens long names; versatile | Needs distinctive letterforms |
| Pictorial mark | A recognizable image, no text | Instant symbol | Hard to land for a new brand |
| Abstract mark | Shapes or lines, no literal object | Ownable, flexible | Takes time to acquire meaning |
| Emblem | Text inside a badge or crest | Authority, heritage | Least scalable; text shrinks with the mark |
| Combination mark | Symbol plus text, separable | Most versatile; the symbol works alone | The symbol must pass on its own first |

A combination mark whose symbol survives alone at favicon size is the safest default for a new
brand: the symbol carries recognition and the type carries the name.

## 2. Negative space, double meaning, visual puns

- Figure-ground: a symbol can hide in the gaps between shapes. Overlap elements and fit them
  together, and design in one color first.
- Simplicity first: the main shape must be readable at a glance. Design in one color initially, and
  add color and typography only after the positive-negative relationship works in monochrome.
- A second layer of meaning (the arrow between the E and x in FedEx) turns negative space into a
  visual pun. Give each concept one primary read (what it is) and at most one hidden read (what
  it means).

Source: The Next Scoop, <https://thenextscoop.com/negative-space-in-logo-design>.

## 3. Simplicity and scalability tests

**Design for 16 px first** (ikonra,
<https://ikonra.com/journal/designing-a-logo-that-works-at-16px>):

- Test the mark at its smallest size first.
- Keep to 2–3 shapes.
- Check that gaps and counters do not close.
- Define symbol-only, wordmark-only and primary lock-ups.

**Simple and flexible** (Smashing Magazine, responsive logos,
<https://smashingmagazine.com/2016/04/logo-design-responsive-websites>):

- "The very best logos are simple and flexible."
- The brain remembers simple forms.
- Test equilibrium by scaling down: busy logos become unreadable and simple ones scale.

**Four contexts** (Smashing Magazine, vital tips,
<https://smashingmagazine.com/2009/08/26/vital-tips-for-effective-logo-design>): is the logo still
effective printed in one color, in reverse color, at the size of a postage stamp, and as large as a
billboard? Start in black and white. The Rand/AIGA principles it quotes: distinctive, memorable,
clear, simple.

**Vector master** (Smashing Magazine, common mistakes,
<https://smashingmagazine.com/2009/06/10-common-mistakes-in-logo-design>): build the master in
vector; raster risks pixelation.

### Test protocol for any concept

1. Real-size 16 px squint test.
2. Solid dark on light and light on dark.
3. Symbol only, with no wordmark or tagline.
4. Strokes of at least about 3 px at the 16 px render, with gaps at least as wide as the stroke.
