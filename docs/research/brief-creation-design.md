# Brief creation that gives design decisions a purpose

Research: 2026-11-10. Design/process research; proposals only. No fixture, production contract, question, threshold or owner label changes.

## Recommendation and evidence limits

Create the brief through a short conversation that establishes a communication problem, then develops visual possibilities. Keep business facts, owner constraints, assumptions and designer proposals visibly separate. Add specificity where it changes a design decision. More adjectives, more ornament and longer briefs are not established routes to better logos.

The current [Rootshare fixture](../../benchmarks/omp/qualification/rootshare-v2/brief.toml) combines a neighborhood seed-library description with the already selected R/seed construction in its summary; its appearance field specifies clay, edges, grain, highlights, side walls and inlay. This is evidence of uneven emphasis, not proof that wording caused the rejected design. The owner rejected Rootshare overall and requested a more specific brief, more detail, and comparison with both Chief logos. Both Chief references have approved craft; no preference between their finishes is established. The existing [case note](rootshare-brief-and-craft.md) records these distinctions.

Three useful primary-source precedents:

- **Problem before solution.** Design Council describes discovering the problem with affected people, defining the challenge, developing different answers and testing small-scale solutions. This supports a conversation that may revise the initial problem statement. It is process guidance, not an experiment proving a particular briefing form improves logo quality. [Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/).
- **Exploration with a concrete job.** Pentagram reports exploring many Slack directions before retaining and retooling the octothorpe to improve reproduction and consistency. Slack itself describes an eleven-color mark dependent on a white background and prescribed angle, with compensating versions producing inconsistent app buttons. These are a designer/client account of one project, not general causal evidence. [Pentagram: Slack](https://www.pentagram.com/work/slack/story), [Slack’s own announcement](https://slack.com/blog/news/say-hello-new-logo).
- **Specificity without ornamental density.** Pentagram describes preserving Mastercard’s existing equity, designing for digital contexts, and building a wider identity from geometry and color. This illustrates why a brief should identify what must survive and where it must work. A young brand cannot assume Mastercard’s accumulated recognition. [Pentagram: Mastercard](https://www.pentagram.com/work/mastercard/story).

All following workflow, worksheet and Rootshare directions are research recommendations to test, not claims demonstrated by those case studies.

## A better brief-creation conversation

Use small rounds of consequential questions. Read back what has been learned after each round; reuse supplied facts instead of making the owner repeat them. Offer options only after a free description, so the agent’s first metaphor does not become the owner’s answer.

1. **Understand the real activity.** “Describe one person using this service: what do they do, and what changes for them?” Follow with “What do people choose instead?” Ask for an actual example or source if differentiation is asserted. Capture the service action rather than converting it immediately into a symbol.
2. **Choose the communication priority.** “At first glance, which impression matters most? What would be a harmful misunderstanding?” Convert broad adjectives into tensions: welcoming but not childish; carefully maintained but not exclusive. Record the owner’s words and the designer’s interpretation separately.
3. **Establish encounters and constraints.** “Where will the identity appear first, and beside what?” Rank the actual uses; identify exact text, existing equity, mandatory colors and forbidden associations. Distinguish “I want an R” from “an R seemed a promising first idea.” A deliberate owner-prescribed symbol is legitimate; it should be an explicit constraint with known tradeoffs.
4. **Read references by role.** For each reference ask “Which visible relationship should we learn from, and which features should stay behind?” Use separate examples for semantic ambition, composition, material and craft when appropriate. A reference is not evidence of audience preference or permission to copy its motif.
5. **Read back the brief.** Present a short business brief, ranked criteria, unresolved assumptions and two or three contrasting concept territories. Ask the owner to correct the consequential points together. Do not ask them to invent bevels, node settings or a complete logo.
6. **Resolve detail after the concept.** For the selected direction, agree a construction and detail plan before expensive finish work. Compare actual previews with the approved intent; reopen the brief only for new information and preserve the revision reason.

Optional questions should earn their place: would a different answer change the concept, composition, material, application or acceptance decision? If not, leave them out. Unknowns that change the central proposition require resolution; minor aesthetic unknowns can remain marked exploration choices.

## Minimum useful brief and reusable worksheet

A brief is ready for exploratory design when it contains a service/activity, a primary audience situation, one communication priority, required encounters, hard constraints and honest unknowns. This is a proposed readiness rule, not an automatic quality score. Complete only relevant fields; a fictional benchmark may deliberately supply less real-world evidence.

| Field | Reusable prompt / record |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Name, pronunciation if relevant, required text; existing identity/equity to retain |
| Business reality | What happens for whom? One concrete service action; source or owner statement |
| Audience encounter | Primary person, situation and place they see the mark; secondary audiences separately |
| Promise and proof | Benefit we want associated with the brand; actual behavior supporting it; unsupported aspirations marked |
| Alternatives and confusion | What users choose instead; two nearby categories or visual clichés to distinguish from |
| Communication hierarchy | One dominant impression; up to two supporting impressions; explicitly lower priorities |
| Tone boundaries | “X, with Y limit”: welcoming without childishness; explain in audience terms |
| Applications | Rank real uses; sizes/backgrounds/context; required lockups or text; proposed uses marked |
| Constraints | Must / prefer / explore / avoid; explain the reason and who established each |
| Reference annotation | Asset, role, visible quality sought, features excluded, owner approval scope |
| Concept latitude | What may vary: literal/abstract, initial/non-initial, material, palette; selected ideas kept separate |
| Acceptance evidence | Actual-size applications, flat and hero consistency, craft comparison, owner review; no rationale-only approval |
| Unknowns | Question, current assumption, consequence if wrong, decision owner |

Label entries **established**, **assumption**, **proposal** or **open**. “Established” means supported by the supplied project record, not independently proven market truth. Keep three distinct artifacts within the working brief: business requirements; proposed concept rationale; selected appearance/construction specification. A concept rationale must not rewrite the service definition to make its own symbol pass.

## What “more detail” should mean

Agree a detail hierarchy rather than an ornament quota:

- **Primary:** silhouette, typography, counter or relationship carrying recognition and the chosen idea; checked in flat form.
- **Secondary:** proportion, spacing, joins and assembly that reward closer inspection and make the object intentional; checked in realistic applications and hero previews.
- **Tertiary:** finish, grain and lighting revealing the chosen material; checked at final hero size. These details may disappear in a favicon.

For each proposed detail record: visible role, purpose, intended scale, construction logic and what happens if removed. A detail can earn its place by improving proportion, readability or material coherence; it need not encode a new business metaphor. Conversely, a generic sprout plus a long explanation does not establish distinctiveness. Do not require an unfamiliar viewer to decode the whole service model from the symbol alone.

## Revised Rootshare example — proposed, not a replacement fixture

**Established from the archived fixture:** Rootshare is a neighborhood seed library; gardeners borrow and contribute seeds to a shared local collection. Tone: grounded, welcoming, careful. Digital uses: favicon, app icon, social avatar. Current intentions: R, terracotta/pale palette and unglazed ceramic treatment. Avoid wax seals, metallic lettering, ribbons and generic sprouts. These are fixture intentions, not proof of independently validated owner mandates.

**Proposed communication brief:**

> Create an identity for Rootshare, a neighborhood seed library used by gardeners borrowing and contributing seeds. Prioritize a welcoming resource held in common, supported by the grounded care of gardening. Develop a distinctive, compact mark that belongs to this name and can work consistently across favicon, app icon and social avatar. Seed imagery may support the idea, but should not reduce the identity to a generic gardening icon. Keep the business purpose separate from the explanation of any selected symbol.

**Assumptions to validate:** Shared-resource character should lead over growth/nature; confusion with a seed retailer is undesirable; an R may be reconsidered; terracotta remains a promising treatment rather than an irreversible mandate. The first two interpretations are plausible positioning choices, not additional service facts.

**Open business questions:** What is distinctive about borrowing/contributing here? Is there a physical local collection? What actually motivates participation? Are seed return, tracking or local provenance part of the service? Do not invent any of these answers. Printed packets and signage remain proposed contexts, not established deliverables.

**Three proposed territories:** A shared chamber built from complementary forms; a contribution/collection relationship; a refined initial-led identity with the service communicated by its surrounding name/context. Each must make a different visual argument. The third deliberately tests whether a nonliteral solution serves better than encoding exchange. Do not select by explanatory prose alone.

**Appearance after selection:** Test a substantial matte ceramic object with deliberately resolved transitions and a single coherent assembly logic. Decide whether a pale accent is a flush insert, recess or applied relief. Record what that accent contributes. Revisit the treatment if it masks the selected relationship or reads primarily as pottery branding. Do not substitute grain for contour refinement.

**Chief reference role:** Compare separately against [original](../../benchmarks/omp/assets/original-seal-2000.png) and [reconstruction](../../benchmarks/omp/assets/reconstruction-rendered-2000.png) for care in form, proportions, hierarchy, edges, construction and finish. Exclude their seal, ribbons, lettering and metallic/blue styling from Rootshare’s identity direction. Neither reference implies the owner prefers greater gloss or greater element count.

## Checkpoints and a practical next experiment

First review the business brief and unresolved assumptions. Then show three materially different flat territories in matched contexts, without their rationales initially; collect unprompted descriptions, relevant confusions and owner reactions. Reveal the rationale afterward and record where it relies on explanation. This is exploratory feedback, not a statistically representative user study.

Select a direction only after reviewing meaning, distinctiveness and required applications. Review its detail/construction plan before final polish. At final review, inspect whole images plus relevant native-size crops; compare craft against both Chief references independently, evaluate fit against Rootshare’s own brief, and obtain fresh owner review. An overall owner rejection remains a rejection without inventing dimensional labels.

Test the workflow on Rootshare and a contrasting brief. Retain the original brief, proposed clarification, owner corrections, alternatives and rejection reasons. Success would be better-supported decisions and accepted actual designs, not a longer brief or higher self-reported confidence. These outcomes have not yet been demonstrated.
