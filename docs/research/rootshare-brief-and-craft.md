## 1. Resolve the brief before polishing the R

The current brief is unusually specific about orange clay, a pale inlay, edge softness and camera-object character, but much less specific about Rootshare's distinguishing behavior. It names neighborhood gardeners and a shared seed collection, then commits immediately to an R protecting a seed. That makes an attractive category/initial solution easy to describe while leaving the sharing idea underdeveloped. This is an inference from the local brief and image, not evidence that a particular replacement symbol will succeed.

The Design Council separates understanding the problem, defining it, developing different answers and testing solutions. Its method supports reopening the problem definition before investing in another finished rendering. [Design Council: Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/).

For the next round, explicitly record:

1. **Distinctive action:** contributing and borrowing maintain one neighborhood collection. Determine whether saving seed for a later season is also a real service behavior; do not invent mandatory return rules, provenance tracking, local seed adaptation, or membership benefits.
2. **Communication priority:** Rootshare should feel like a shared neighborhood resource, with seeds as the domain. Explain what must distinguish it from a gardening retailer, nursery, conservation charity or pottery studio.
3. **Audience situation:** a gardener must identify the service in a small avatar or app icon; the hero may show finer construction. Printed seed packets or collection labels are useful proposed contexts, not established deliverables until confirmed.
4. **Visual proposition:** every candidate must synthesize the name and the shared-resource action, rather than place an unrelated ornament beside an R. An R is a candidate constraint to revisit, not an automatic success criterion.
5. **Evidence for material choice:** retain terracotta only if its grounded, cared-for character supports the identity. It does not literally establish sharing or library behavior. State that relationship as a design rationale rather than a brand fact.

Suggested working brief paragraph, pending authoring validation:

> Rootshare is a neighborhood seed library where gardeners borrow and contribute to one shared local collection. Create a welcoming, carefully made identity that expresses a resource held in common. Explore a recognizable R only where its construction also conveys contribution, exchange or shared shelter. Preserve one strong small-size silhouette. In the hero, use unglazed terracotta and a restrained pale ceramic detail to make that same idea tangible through deliberate joints, recesses and edges. Avoid generic sprout decoration, retail-gardening cues and borrowed Chief styling.

Known fixture facts are the name, neighborhood seed-library purpose, gardeners borrowing/contributing, three digital uses, current tone/palette and current material intention in the archived brief. The shared-resource emphasis, any interpretation of ceramic as communal care, and all proposed visual constructions are design assumptions to test. The task does not establish return obligations, cataloguing, local provenance, printed deliverables or a mandatory R. Do not represent them as product facts.

Proposed non-negotiables: keep one coherent flat/hero identity; show a seed-library/shared-resource proposition beyond a category icon; preserve small-size recognition; use intentional material/edge construction; avoid wax seals, metallic lettering, ribbons and generic sprouts; meet both independent craft comparisons without replacing Rootshare's identity with Chief styling. Terracotta and the pale insert are current fixture intentions, not globally prescribed quality rules.

This is a proposed clarification, not an instruction to silently replace the archived brief.

## 2. Add design decisions, not arbitrary ornament

Pentagram's first-party Mastercard case study connects geometry to the idea of connection, builds the wider identity from the mark's elements, and describes extensive color testing across backgrounds. It demonstrates that specificity can live in relationships and calibration even when the symbol remains simple. It does not establish that an unfamiliar Rootshare monogram will communicate without context. [Pentagram: Mastercard](https://www.pentagram.com/work/mastercard).

Author three genuinely different flat studies before another final render:

| Study | Brand hypothesis to test | Purposeful detail opportunity | Risk to reject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared counter | Two complementary portions of the R make one protected seed space | One deliberate meeting or handoff where the parts depend on each other | A split letter whose exchange explanation exists only in its caption |
| Contributing forms | Distinct forms contribute to a single stable initial or seed chamber | Repeated curvature or interlocking termination gives the parts a shared grammar | Puzzle-piece or corporate partnership cliché |
| Seed-library construction | A recognizable storage/collection relationship is fused with the name | Aperture, retaining lip or inset surface makes keeping and sharing tangible | Literal container illustration that loses the R or resembles packaging |

These are exploration directions, not selected concepts. Compare each with the unchanged R in one color and ask what is communicated without the rationale. Reject concepts that only become brand-specific after reading the explanation.

For any retained direction, write a short detail ledger: feature, brand purpose, visible role, minimum intended size, geometry/material method, and deletion test. Example: a pale insert may articulate the shared chamber and its relationship to the outer form; if removing it changes neither meaning nor hierarchy, its present outline may be decoration. Detail can instead mean more resolved R proportions, a controlled bowl-to-leg junction, optical counter spacing, an intentionally seated insert, or edge profiles differentiated by function. No source or observation establishes that adding more polygons, grain, seams or lettering guarantees craft.

Use three levels of purposeful detail, with deletion tests at each level:

1. **Primary semantic detail:** the outer form/counter relationship must communicate the selected sharing or collection proposition and survive in one color at small sizes. This level carries recognition.
2. **Secondary construction detail:** a deliberate seating joint, counter lip or complementary part boundary explains how the ceramic object is made and reinforces the same proposition at avatar/hero scale. It must not become the only expression of the idea.
3. **Tertiary material detail:** restrained grain, small roughness variation and controlled edge light give credible clay at native final scale. These may disappear at favicon size and must never obscure the first two levels.

Keep the [shared-master contract](../adr/0001-motif-plus-treatment.md): essential symbol contours and semantic divisions belong in the motif. The hero may express depth and finish, but must not introduce the only evidence of sharing through geometry absent from the flat mark. Do not force microtexture or secondary hero detail to remain visible at favicon scale.

## 3. Make the ceramic object convincing through construction

The local sculpt already constructs closed rounded meshes and checks watertightness, winding and positive volume. The scene uses a clay material with bump plus a porcelain accent. Those facts establish authoring intent and some geometry checks; they do not establish visual craft. In particular, the pale detail is authored as a thin separate solid on the face, so its visual integration deserves direct inspection. [Sculpt preparation](../../benchmarks/omp/qualification/rootshare-v2/concept/prepare_sculpt.py), [scene recipe](../../benchmarks/omp/qualification/rootshare-v2/concept/scene.toml).

Prioritized authoring experiments:

- **Contour and junction first:** resolve the R shoulder, seed apex, counter spacing and bowl/leg join in front view. Inspect all transitions under an untextured diagnostic material. Distinguish a silhouette problem from a normal/shading problem before smoothing it.
- **Specify the insert's fabrication logic:** decide whether pale ceramic is flush inlay, recessed slip, or raised applied clay. Make its seating, thickness, corners and material boundary agree with that choice. A bright outline hovering ambiguously above an aperture is weak evidence of an inlay.
- **Give edges a hierarchy:** outer handling edge, inner counter wall and inset boundary can have different but coordinated profiles. Blender exposes bevel width, segments, custom profiles and intersection controls; these are independent modeling choices, not one universal smoothing value. This supports investigating the actual profile and join, rather than increasing render samples to hide it. The existing custom sculpt need not be replaced by a bevel modifier. [Blender 5.1 manual: Bevel](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.1/modeling/modifiers/generate/bevel.html).
- **Tune material response against the intended matte ceramic:** Principled BSDF models diffuse/dielectric reflection separately from metal and coat, and roughness controls reflection spread. Use those controls to develop a readable matte object; turning reflections off entirely or making the face glossy is not a substitute for material specificity. [Blender: Principled BSDF](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/render/shader_nodes/shader/principled.html).
- **Use grain as a supporting scale:** Blender's Bump node perturbs normals from height; it does not supply silhouette geometry. Use it for fine restrained clay response, not to conceal wavy contours or invent construction. Check the feature at final delivered pixel scale and after downsampling. [Blender 5.1 manual: Bump](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.1/render/shader_nodes/displacement/bump.html).
- **Light to reveal decisions:** test one broad key plus controlled grazing/fill contribution so the face remains matte while counter walls, edge transitions and insert contact remain legible. This is a proposed experiment, not a proven lighting recipe. Compare neutral diagnostic and final brand backgrounds; do not accept a material merely because black or transparency makes its edge dramatic.

Keep render baselines and GPU execution requirements unchanged. Neither samples, Metal provenance nor denoiser smoothness proves a design idea or good material construction. The [architecture](../architecture.md) explicitly separates those dimensions.

## 4. Validate against both Chief finishes without copying them

1. Freeze the revised brief and each candidate revision. Preserve the rejected image and its owner feedback. Record reference identities independently; do not silently substitute a new render for either Chief image.
2. At the flat stage, inspect actual-size 16/32 px, monochrome positive/reverse and larger front views. Establish the R/seed relationship and whether the new shared-resource idea is visible. Use unprompted descriptions where possible, then assess intended meaning separately; recognition of a seed alone is insufficient evidence for the whole proposition.
3. At preview, compare each candidate separately to **each** Chief reference in both orders, using matched canvas treatment and clear independent intended-finish notes. Ask about contour/join control, surface construction, purposeful details, edge refinement, material distinction, proportion, hierarchy and lighting. Judge care relative to the specified ceramic treatment, not similarity to blue wax or gold.
4. Inspect the final whole images and native-pixel crops of the counter apex, bowl/leg junction, stem foot, outer curves and inlay boundary. Give the references corresponding detail views as well. The displayed whole-image reduction does not resolve the exact cause of small marks or shading changes.
5. Separate own-brief fit, intended-appearance fit, technical image acceptance, reference-relative execution and preference. A candidate can improve on the rejected R and still fail either Chief floor; record that result plainly. An average across references must not hide a failure against one.
6. Retain existing Jev eligibility calls, essential bands, uncertainty handling, owner-rejection veto and image/input binding described in [architecture](../architecture.md). Do not alter questions or thresholds to manufacture acceptance. This research proposes authoring experiments and review evidence, not a new scoring implementation.
7. Obtain fresh owner review of actual candidate pixels and the clarified brief. An unsettled or rejected outcome remains unresolved; no positive-owner override, research rationale or model preference can manufacture owner approval.

TypeSafe recommends concrete, atomic judgments, relevant structured state, explicit decision logic and routing uncertainty to review. They do not prove which factor caused the present answers. The additional whole-image-only control already failed to resolve the Chief disagreement, so merely removing detail context is not a supported repair. [TypeSafe: building with System One](https://docs.typesafe.ai/concepts/how-to-build-with-system-one.md), [model jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness.md), [retained context control](../../benchmarks/omp/validation/issue-2/follow-up/whole-image-control/README.md).

Proposed pre-authoring review, separate from final image eligibility:

- Does the business brief specify a service action, audience and communication priority without naming the already-chosen geometry as proof of distinctiveness?
- Does each proposed visual relationship have a rationale tied to that action, with unsupported business claims explicitly marked as assumptions?
- Does the detail ledger say what each secondary feature contributes and at which usage scale it matters?
- Has the physical construction been chosen explicitly: flush insert, recessed surface or applied relief, with compatible edge and contact decisions?
- Are business requirements frozen before motif authoring, so a later fit judgment cannot be satisfied simply by rewriting the brief around the rendered result?

These are research proposals, not new production Jev questions or automatic acceptance checks. Any implementation must keep wording in `logo_maker/jev/questions.py`, change the contract first, and validate on untouched cases. A logo need not explain the entire seed-library service to an unfamiliar viewer. Assess the specified visible relationship, recognizability and relevant confusion separately; do not demand that every business fact be decoded from the symbol alone.

Native-pixel inspection of the retained lower-left candidate tile confirms small dark/pinched marks at the stem foot and an uneven-looking transition at the bowl/leg junction. This is an observation, not a diagnosis of their geometry or shading cause. ## Recommended next investment

First develop the brief's shared-resource proposition and three distinct flat constructions. Then resolve the selected motif's proportions and ceramic assembly, especially the counter and pale detail. Only after those decisions survive inspection should the next final render test fine grain, lighting and reference-relative finish. This sequence targets the visible semantic and construction gaps without treating ornament, metal or gloss as quality.
