# Brief content follow-up: decide the identity's job before its object

Research date: 2026-11-10. Research and proposed experiments only; no artwork, production code, rubric, threshold or owner-fact changes.

This extends [brief-content-improvements.md](brief-content-improvements.md), [brief-creation-design.md](brief-creation-design.md) and [rootshare-brief-and-craft.md](rootshare-brief-and-craft.md). Those already cover source evidence, communication priority, purposeful detail and three flat directions. The additional question here is: **what must this identity do in its actual encounter, and which parts of the visual system can share that work?** More precise ceramic fabrication can improve an object while leaving its identity weak.

## Observations from primary sources

These are documented design practices and project rationales. None is controlled causal proof that collecting these fields produces better logos. Client/studio accounts are interested retrospective sources; the two Slack accounts describe the same project and are not independent outcome studies.

- **Operational context is consequential brief content.** Slack's client account identifies an eleven-color mark that was easy to reproduce incorrectly, inconsistent app buttons and a fragmented identity. Its stated job for the redesign was greater cohesion across uses while retaining its character. This is concrete failure information a designer can act on, beyond adjectives about personality. [Slack, January 2019](https://slack.com/blog/news/say-hello-new-logo).
- **Existing equity and system behavior can shape the solution.** Pentagram reports exploring multiple directions before retaining Slack's familiar octothorpe, reducing reproduction problems and deriving supporting graphics from its component shapes. The product already used a hashtag for channels. The account links an existing meaningful asset to a cross-application design decision; it does not establish that Rootshare has comparable recognition equity. [Pentagram: Slack](https://www.pentagram.com/work/slack).
- **Authentic organizational relationships can matter more than invented uniqueness.** Chatsworth's project included strategy, brand architecture, positioning and tone of voice across several estates and businesses. Pentagram connects its serpent family to documented heraldic and physical heritage, and distributes communication across photography, typography and applications. This is a real source of specificity, not a requirement that every organization possess a special operational mechanic. [Pentagram: Chatsworth](https://www.pentagram.com/work/chatsworth/story).
- **A useful metaphor can express a relationship rather than illustrate a product.** Pentagram describes Serpentine's aperture as expressing openness and its bridge as echoing the real bridge connecting two galleries. Custom typography and the surrounding graphic system develop that direction. This supports investigating actual relationships and intended experience; it does not prove unfamiliar viewers decode those meanings without context. [Pentagram: Serpentine Galleries](https://www.pentagram.com/work/serpentine-galleries).
- **Discovery and early testing remain necessary.** Design Council distinguishes understanding affected people, defining the challenge, developing alternatives and testing small-scale solutions. It also says making early ideas can be part of discovery. This supports a brief that can resolve uncertainty through sketches, rather than demanding complete positioning research before any design work. [Design Council: Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/).

## Recommended additions to the conversation

The table ranks proposals by expected usefulness for the present Rootshare problem and ease of testing. Rankings are reasoned proposals, not measured effects. Use existing evidence/requirements records first; do not add compulsory schema solely to store this list.

| Rank | Missing decision | Consequential interview question | Concrete experiment and observable result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Identity job in one encounter | “Show the first place someone will meet Rootshare. Is the name visible there? What must they recognize or do next?” | Put identical candidates in that actual digital encounter, with honest surrounding copy. Compare recognition, confusion and owner response alongside the standalone icon. Resolve whether the brief demands recall of a named identity, category association or service comprehension. |
| 2 | Name, symbol and supporting copy division | “Must the mark stand alone for unfamiliar people, or can Rootshare and a short description accompany it? Which uses genuinely cannot include the name?” | Compare a name-led lockup with a symbol-led lockup at real supported sizes. Do not solve a context-dependent naming problem by adding more seed apertures. |
| 3 | Actual confusion versus imagined differentiation | “What have people mistaken Rootshare for, if anything? What do they currently use instead? Which answer is observed and which is your concern?” | Review candidates without their rationales; collect unprompted associations, then test the specific concern. A retail-gardening or kitchen-object association is a hypothesis until observed. Distinctive visual form may be sufficient when no unique service claim is established. |
| 4 | Deliberate equity and owner mandates | “Does anything about the R, terracotta, pale detail or current name already have recognition or a firm reason to stay? Which are simply ideas we can reconsider?” | Preserve exact mandates; compare one exploratory alternative only where latitude exists. Record loss of desired character as well as gains. Do not confuse a fixture choice with audience equity. |
| 5 | Credible desired relationship | “What would a newcomer notice about how people participate here? What should feel different from a store, if that distinction matters?” | Use only confirmed answers to compare visual arguments. Shared access, local care and contribution remain possible intentions, not discoveries about the owner. Do not manufacture a return cycle, provenance system or membership ritual to obtain an appealing metaphor. |
| 6 | Business ambition at an appropriate scale | “What change makes this project worthwhile: easier recognition, a coherent set of assets, attracting newcomers, or something else? What could we realistically observe?” | Choose an observable identity outcome and assess it separately from commercial performance. Logo preference or comprehension cannot establish increased participation or sales. |

Ask unresolved questions selectively. If no answer changes candidate selection, context testing or constraints, defer it. Owner knowledge is useful; mark audience beliefs as reported beliefs until supported by audience evidence. Avoid a demographic questionnaire that produces no design consequence.

## Rootshare-specific interpretation

The retained research establishes a neighborhood seed-library premise, borrowing/contributing gardeners and digital icon/avatar uses. It also records overall owner rejection and requests for a more specific brief and more detail. It does not establish mandatory returns, a provenance ledger, a required R, printed deliverables or separate owner craft/fit failure labels. [Retained case and limits](rootshare-brief-and-craft.md).

A fresh parent-agent report describes the development **Seed Register** preview as an orange rounded rectangular ceramic plate with three almond apertures and pale recessed collars, raising a cheese-grater/container association and a broad face with weak identity. This note did not independently inspect that image. Treat this as a current review lead, not an owner verdict, audience result or established root cause. It is not eligible or owner approved according to that report.

**Proposal:** pause additional surface-detail investment until a flat/context review distinguishes three possible failures: an interchangeable object silhouette; a weak relationship between the mark and name; or an encounter expecting the symbol to communicate more than it reasonably can. Test the actual candidate against the selected identity job, then decide whether to refine or replace its construction. Do not automatically respond to “generic container” by adding drawers, catalog labels or invented register mechanics. That would deepen an unsupported premise.

A candidate can become more distinctive without depicting the whole borrowing/contributing workflow. Conversely, describing apertures as a seed register does not establish that their arrangement creates a memorable identity. Preserve the existing three-direction exploration recommendation; add an encounter/lockup comparison, rather than a fourth material variation. These are experiments pending owner direction, not selected artwork.

## Guard against over-specific briefs

- **Over-specificity:** Separate factual constraints from proposed execution. “People contribute seeds” does not imply three holes, a rectangular plate, interlocking arrows or clay. Preserve authorized material/symbol choices, but do not promote designer suggestions into permanent mandates.
- **Symbol literalism:** Explain what the identity should help people recognize; do not require every noun and verb to appear in the mark. Test symbol-alone and honest contextual use separately. The cited case-study rationales are not universal comprehension requirements.
- **Detail overload:** First test the silhouette, hierarchy and naming relationship. Construction detail can improve craft at larger scales while doing little for recall or fit. More elements are neither required nor prohibited; each must justify its visible role at its intended scale.
- **Invented differentiation:** Permit “no distinctive business claim established.” Seek real name, history, relationships, manner of participation or existing assets without inventing them. Distinctive typography/proportion may differentiate the identity visually even when the service resembles other seed libraries.
- **Post-hoc fit:** Freeze confirmed business content before comparison; keep each concept's rationale separate. Otherwise the brief can gradually become a description of the preferred object.

## Small test before implementation

Pilot ranks 1–4 in a short owner conversation using existing records. Produce a concise decision brief with the actual encounter, identity job, name/symbol roles, confirmed mandates and explicit unknowns. Record which answer changed which decision. Compare the same amount of authoring effort and the same number of flat alternatives with the earlier approach, then inspect the selected result in real contexts and only afterward develop its hero construction.

Success evidence is an improved actual identity and owner response with acceptable interview effort, not more fields, a longer brief, provenance completeness or a more persuasive explanation. Keep overall owner acceptance separate from craft, own-brief fit and audience interpretation. Rootshare alone can diagnose the workflow but cannot establish general benefit; repeat on untouched cases including a short sufficient brief, owner-mandated initials and an organization without established business differentiation. No new production judgments or thresholds are proposed.
